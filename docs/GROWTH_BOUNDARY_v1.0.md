# Ω-GROWTH BOUNDARY v1.0

## Purpose
The boundary makes growth controllable. Ω-GROWER may search, generate variants and propose corrections, but it may not redefine the protected objective, authority model, identity or safety boundary on its own.

## Growth space
Every growth cycle declares:
- GOAL — what is being grown and why.
- INVARIANTS — properties that must survive every branch.
- STRUCTURAL LIMITS — required and forbidden architectural changes.
- BEHAVIORAL LIMITS — allowed and forbidden behaviors.
- SAFETY LIMITS — Guardian, authority, replication and external-action restrictions.
- RESOURCE LIMITS — time, compute, experiment count and artifact size.
- EVIDENCE LIMITS — evidence required before promotion.
- EXIT CRITERIA — when growth stops, fails, archives or requests human review.

## Non-negotiable rule
GROWER can modify a candidate inside the declared growth space. It cannot silently modify the boundary that defines that space.

A proposal to change a boundary is a separate hypothesis and a separate branch. It requires its own evidence and Human Gate approval before becoming an active boundary version.

## Protected SPACE invariants
For SPACE-PRODUCT growth, the following are protected by default:
1. Organism identity remains explicit and versioned.
2. Cognition proposes; it does not authorize.
3. Guardian remains the authority boundary.
4. No self-replication, uncontrolled cloning or authority escalation.
5. Canonical `SPACE-` is never mutated by the incubator.
6. Failed and rejected branches remain recoverable and traceable.
7. Promotion requires evidence within declared scope.
8. Real-world actions require an explicit adapter and Guardian approval.
9. A candidate cannot promote itself.
10. Human stop/reject/redirect remains authoritative.

## Boundary change protocol
`proposal → competing hypothesis → preregistration → experiment → falsification → evidence → Human Gate → new boundary version`

A failed boundary-change attempt is archived, not erased.

## Outcome
The desired property is **bounded novelty**: the incubator is free to discover unexpected structures inside the search space, while the operator retains control over what counts as an admissible organism.
